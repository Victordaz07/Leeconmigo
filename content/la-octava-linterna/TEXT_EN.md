# The Eighth Lantern

## Page 1

Every autumn, the neighborhood lit paper lanterns in the square. Luz knew their order by heart: the first seven lit the food stalls; the last one had a blue star and glowed beside the path.

That afternoon, Luz counted once. Then she counted again. Seven lights, and one empty hook.

“Maybe it broke,” Nico said.

Luz studied the hook. It was clean and firm. No one seemed to have tried to hang anything there. Around them, music began as people set up the tables.

## Page 2

Nico thought someone might have moved the lantern. Luz nearly ran off to search for it, but stopped. A missing lantern did not, by itself, explain what had happened.

They asked the festival organizer. She looked up from a box of battery lights.

“Doña Sabina brings it every year,” she said. “I haven't seen her today.”

Luz knew Sabina well enough to greet her in the street, though they had rarely talked. She remembered Sabina staying until the end to watch the lights from the edge of the square.

## Page 3

On the community center board were photos from past festivals. Luz found one of Sabina smiling beside a honey-colored lantern. Its blue star stood out even in the old picture.

“That's the one,” Nico said. “Let's ask if she has it.”

Luz felt uneasy about the words. They sounded as if Sabina owed them something. She didn't owe an explanation to two curious children.

“We can ask how she is and whether she'd like to come,” Luz replied.

They walked along the side lane, which reached Sabina's home without the square's steps.

## Page 4

Sabina's window faced the street. On a table behind the glass sat the lantern. Its blue star faced outward. It was neither broken nor lost.

Luz felt she had solved the mystery. But when she saw Sabina sitting by the table, the question changed. If the lantern was there, why wasn't Sabina at the festival?

Nico pointed to the object. Luz gently lowered his hand.

“Let's knock first,” she said. “And listen.”

Sabina opened the door after the bell rang.

## Page 5

Sabina invited them to talk from the doorway. She explained that the walk to the square had become tiring for her lately. She had thought of asking for help, but didn't want everyone to interrupt their preparations. So she left the lantern on the table.

“From here I can hear a little of the music,” she said with a smile. “I can't see the lights.”

Luz looked at the cane by the chair, then at the lantern. She had come looking for an object. Now she understood someone was missing.

“We could take the lantern for you,” Nico offered. Sabina thanked him, but did not answer at once.

## Page 6

“I like seeing it light up among the people,” Sabina finally said. “But I don't want to make everyone change the festival.”

Luz leaned against the doorframe, leaving the way clear. She could not promise anything without asking the people who planned the celebration.

“What if we ask them to begin on this lane? It's level, and the square is at the end. You could watch from here or join us for part of it, whichever you prefer.”

Sabina thought for a moment.

“Ask them,” she said. “If it works for everyone, I'd like to try.”

## Page 7

The organizer listened to the idea. Moving the start was not enough: they had to check the path and how to hang the lanterns safely. Adults looked over the lane. The lights ran on batteries and could hang from sturdy supports beside the lane.

Nico sketched the new route without blocking anyone's doorway. Luz went back to tell Sabina the plan and asked whether it still suited her. Sabina chose to sit at her door.

As night fell, neighbors began gathering in the side lane.

## Page 8

Seven lanterns lit up, one after another. The eighth, with its blue star, glowed beside Sabina's door. She watched the paper turn golden from within. Nico stopped counting and simply looked.

“I thought a light was missing,” Luz said softly.

“And you asked about me,” Sabina replied.

Later, some neighbors walked toward the square while others stayed to talk in the lane. Luz looked at the complete row. The empty hook had been a clue, but only listening to Sabina had shown them what to do with it.

## Questions

- What did Luz think when she saw the empty hook, and how did her question change at the window?
- Why did Luz choose to listen before taking the lantern?
- Who made the decisions about the new route?
- What does Luz's final thought about the clue mean to you?
