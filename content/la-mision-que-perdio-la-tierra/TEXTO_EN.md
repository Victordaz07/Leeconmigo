# The Mission That Lost Earth

Elián dreams of becoming an astronaut

## 1. The helmet that felt too big

Elián had been telling everyone he would become an astronaut. At home he knew the planets by name and had built a lunar rover out of cardboard.

The hard part was different: whenever someone asked him to lead, his hands shook. He did not want his friend Asha to notice.

That morning they came to the neighborhood space center for a simulated mission. Inside a room of screens, engineer Nora showed them a Moon landscape, a small four-wheeled rover, and a model of Earth hanging above the horizon.

Everything would happen inside the simulator; no one was launching into space. Their task was to guide the rover to a sunlit area before lunar night reached the crater.

Elián received the map. Asha would manage radio calls and observations.

## 2. The first signal

The room dimmed, and the floor gave a gentle vibration to imitate a spacecraft. On the central screen the rover rolled down a gray slope.

Earth was a small blue disc between two mountains. Nora explained that the rover could receive radio commands when it could “see” Earth or an orbiting relay satellite.

Asha checked the signal: one pulse, one reply, and a picture of the path. Everything worked.

Elián had to choose between a long, level route and a shortcut along the crater rim. The shortcut saved time.

Asha wanted to check a rise marked on the map, but Elián said he had already worked it out. In fact, he had only looked at distance.

He pressed the short route. The rover turned.

## 3. The crater rim

At first the shortcut seemed perfect. The rover crossed low rocks and sent pictures of tracks in the dust.

Elián sat taller. Then a crater wall began to rise across the virtual window.

Earth slipped behind it and vanished. The radio returned a dry click with no picture.

Asha tried again. Nothing.

On the map the rover was still heading toward shadow. Elián wanted to order it to turn, but Nora pointed to the console: without a signal, a new command could not arrive.

Asha indicated the rise she had wanted to check. Elián recognized it.

The route was shorter because it ran behind a high wall. Earth had not been lost; they had lost a clear line to speak with it.

## 4. No answer comes back

Nora said the simulation would continue. They could pause if anyone felt uncomfortable, but the exercise was to understand the problem before acting.

Elián opened and closed his hands. He considered saying the screen must be broken.

Asha enlarged the last pictures. The final one still showed a narrow strip of bright sky.

Perhaps the rover was close to leaving the shadow. Nora asked what they knew for certain.

They knew where Earth vanished from the picture, which route Elián had chosen, and that the rover still had power. They did not know its exact position now.

Elián drew a breath. “I chose the shortcut without checking the height,” he admitted.

Asha did not celebrate his mistake. She pulled up a chair and asked him to look for another way to regain the signal together.

## 5. A ball between two voices

Nora paused the crater view and brought out two balls: blue for Earth, gray for the Moon. Asha set a tiny marker on the gray ball.

From there the Moon itself blocked the blue one. Elián moved the marker toward the rim, and Earth came back into view.

Nora placed a little satellite high on a wire. If the relay could see both marker and Earth, it could carry a message between them.

But it was orbiting; the window would be brief. Asha smiled because the map finally made sense.

Elián could see his mistake in three dimensions too. Asking for help had not made the problem larger.

It had made the shape of it visible.

## 6. The satellite will not wait

They switched the simulator on again. A silver point moved slowly across the top of the screen: the relay.

Nora showed them that it would soon reach a place where it could see both Earth and the rover. Elián suggested sending every possible command when it appeared.

Asha asked which should come first. If the rover received conflicting messages at once, it might use power turning back and forth.

They prepared one instruction: stop, turn toward a gentle slope, and wait for confirmation. Elián read the map aloud; Asha checked the slope against the photographs.

Nora did not do the work for them. She reminded them they could still pause the simulation.

Both chose to continue, now with a shared plan.

## 7. A message too early

Elián readied the instruction and held a finger above Send. As the satellite approached the crater, he pressed.

The console showed the message leaving, but no confirmation came back. Asha checked its path.

They had sent it before the relay could see the rover. The command had not been saved for later.

Elián felt his face grow hot. This time he did not pretend everything was fine.

He noted when he had sent the message and asked Asha what landmark they could use. She chose the shadow of a little mountain crossing the screen.

When it touched a round rock, the relay would have the right view. They practiced calling that moment without pressing any button.

## 8. The shadow and the window

The satellite completed another orbit. Asha tracked the edge of the shadow while Elián kept their agreed instruction ready.

Nora watched the gauges without intervening. As the shadow touched the round rock, Asha said, “Now.” Elián sent the command once.

Silence. A small indicator confirmed that the relay had received it; the rover still had to get it.

The simulator included that delay so no one expected an instant reply across such a distance. Elián wanted to press again.

Asha placed the notebook between his hand and the button, without scolding him. They counted together while the satellite moved on.

The rover’s battery continued to fall on the map. Elián discovered that waiting could be part of teamwork too.

## 9. A new photograph

A blurry image arrived. The rover had stopped beside three dark stones, exactly where the map suggested.

Elián found the gentle slope, and Asha checked that it led out of shadow. During the next relay window they sent one command to move forward.

The rover climbed slowly. Sunlight touched its antenna, then its power panels.

The battery stopped falling. Above the crater rim the small blue disc of Earth appeared again, unmistakable.

Nora ended the simulation and switched on the real room lights. Elián looked at Asha.

They had made mistakes and still completed the mission. He had wanted to lead like someone who never doubted himself.

Now he was proud of saying what he did not know.

## 10. The future fair

The following week Elián and Asha brought a crater model to the school’s careers fair. They set out a gray ball, a blue one, and a wire satellite so visitors could test when a message could travel.

Elián told them about his wrong turn too. He did not hide it behind the happy ending.

A girl with two low braids and mint-green overalls stopped at their table. Her name was Abril, and she wanted to become an architect.

Would a Moon shelter belong in the shade to keep it cool? Elián did not know the whole answer.

Asha said they would need to study power, temperature, and communication. Abril sketched three possible sites in her notebook.

They agreed to compare ideas another day. The fair became more interesting when another person’s question opened a new path.

## 11. A hard question

A younger visitor asked if Elián had been afraid when Earth vanished from the screen. Elián glanced at Nora, who was talking with another family, and then at Asha.

He could pretend it had only been a game. “Yes,” he said.

“I thought everyone expected me to know what to do.” The child examined the cardboard crater and moved a marker behind the gray ball. Earth disappeared from that viewpoint too.

Elián showed him how to place the relay where it could see both sides. Abril came back with her notebook and listened.

When the visitor connected the two views, he smiled. Elián realized that speaking about fear did not spoil the adventure.

It let someone else imagine being there and finding a way out.

## 12. Tomorrow’s route

As the fair closed, Elián packed away the wire satellite carefully. Asha asked whether he still wanted to be an astronaut after so many problems.

“More than before,” he said. He no longer thought only about launching and looking down at Earth.

He wanted to learn to read difficult maps, listen to a crew, and ask questions when a piece was missing. Nora reminded them they could return to the space center for another simulation if they planned the route together.

Abril stopped by to say goodbye and showed them a fresh sketch: a shelter with one window toward the sky and another toward a teammate. Elián laughed.

His next mission did not have a map yet, but he knew whom he would ask to check it.
