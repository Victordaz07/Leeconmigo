# The Exit Nobody Counted

Imani learns to look after her team

## 1. The wrong race

Imani thought a firefighter had to be the first person to reach everything. At the station open house she was first onto the red engine, first to try on a practice helmet, and first to touch the table where Captain Solís had prepared an exercise.

Her little brother Damián arrived behind her, holding a red paper dragon. Teacher Farah had brought several children to prepare for the school careers fair.

Solís showed them a cardboard model of the community center: two exit doors and a tree outside. “Where does a safe route end?” he asked.

Imani set a marker at the door. The captain moved it to the tree.

That was where they should meet and tell the adults who had come out. Imani thought this would be easy.

She had not tried it with all the children moving at once.

## 2. Two doors, one tree

Solís placed colored markers in the model rooms. One exit stood near the stage; the other led to a side courtyard.

Farah asked each child to find two possible paths from the meeting room. Damián set his dragon beside a table and moved a marker toward the nearest door.

Imani chose a shortcut through a hallway that looked wide in the model, but Solís closed it with a cardboard piece: a planned route might become unusable. She found the other door.

The captain was not teaching them to enter a fire; they were practicing how to leave and gather outside. Imani sketched both routes on a folded page.

She circled the tree. “And if someone is missing, tell emergency responders,” Farah explained.

“Do not go back to search.” Damián asked to draw his dragon too. Imani gave him a corner.

## 3. The dragon stays behind

The station had a practice route made of fabric curtains and cardboard doors. There was no fire or smoke.

Colored flags showed which hallway to imagine blocked. When the exercise signal sounded, Imani rushed ahead.

She used the correct exit and saw Farah with Damián by the courtyard tree. Then her brother held up his empty hands: his dragon was still on a table inside.

Imani turned to fetch it. Solís, who was supervising, stopped her before she went back.

“In a real emergency nobody goes back for an object,” he reminded her. Imani felt every eye on her.

Damián was safe, but his face crumpled when he remembered the dragon. “I can make you another,” she told him.

The words were difficult. She did not want to be first out if it meant forgetting something important; she was learning that a person mattered more than a toy.

## 4. The number that is missing

When the exercise ended, Solís did not ask who had been fastest. He handed Farah a list of participants and asked her to check it at the meeting place.

They counted once. A marker was missing, not a child: a volunteer had used the second door and was waiting beside another tree.

The surprise had been arranged for the exercise. An adult spoke with that group and brought the volunteer to the agreed spot.

Imani understood that “everyone is outside” was not enough if nobody knew where each person was. Farah noted the drill time so the community-center adults could practice too.

Imani tucked her folded map into a pocket. The following week they would hold a preparation evening there before the school fair.

She promised to show Damián the right tree. He raised his dragon, returned after the exercise, and said he would learn it as well.

## 5. Boxes beside the door

At preparation night the community center was full of tables. Children rehearsed how to show their projects to visitors.

Imani arranged a small two-door model at her table while Damián flew his dragon between empty chairs. Farah supervised the setup.

A folding display and several boxes ended up too close to the side exit. Imani pointed them out.

Farah spoke with the adults in charge, and they moved the boxes to leave the way clear. Imani expected to feel as though she had saved the day, but she had simply noticed a detail before it caused trouble.

Later volunteers shifted the display to make room for a group arriving late. Nobody noticed that one leg stood crooked beside the passage.

Imani was cutting out markers and did not see it. The plan remained in her pocket; meanwhile the room kept changing.

## 6. A real alarm

The alarm sounded while they were still setting up tables. Imani looked up, thinking at first of the station exercise.

Farah glanced at a closed storage room; a small gray wisp came from its upper edge. The teacher told the children to leave everything and go with her to the exit.

An adult called emergency services from outside. Imani left her half-built cardboard fire engine.

Damián set his dragon on the table so he could take a younger child’s hand. Nobody opened the storage-room door or tried to investigate the smoke.

People moved without racing and without going back for their things. The alarm was not a game this time.

Imani felt her legs wanting to go faster than Farah’s. She stayed with the group and took out the folded map only to remember where they were meant to meet.

## 7. The hallway changes

When they turned toward the side exit, Farah saw the folding display leaning over. People passing it had shifted it, and now it narrowed the hallway.

She stopped the group before the obstruction. The smoke was distant, but nobody knew whether it would stay that way.

Farah pointed to the other clear exit and led the children along the alternative route they had practiced. Imani recognized the bend in her map.

She did not push the display or direct anyone on her own; she walked with Farah and Damián toward the door. Another adult volunteer guided people from a different room out of the side exit, away from the display.

The two groups reached outdoors by different paths. The building had changed since the cardboard plan, but the practice had given them more than one way out.

Imani put away the map as soon as she reached the courtyard.

## 8. One name without an answer

Farah brought her group to the agreed tree and began counting. Imani looked for Damián’s blue sweatshirt.

He had been beside her until the door, but outside another adult had guided the younger children toward the opposite side of the yard. She could not see him.

She remembered the dragon on the table and felt an urge to go in and search for her brother. She did not.

She went to Farah and gave Damián’s name, clothing, and the last place she had seen him. Captain Solís had just arrived with the fire crew.

He listened and asked another adult to check the group from the second exit while his colleagues attended to the building. No one assumed Damián was inside, but no one crossed his name off the list either.

Imani waited under the tree with empty hands.

## 9. The other group

A reply came over the radio: Damián was with the volunteer who had used the other door. He had stayed with the younger child whose hand he held.

The volunteer knew the meeting point and brought him to Farah without leaving the other children alone. Damián ran the last few steps toward Imani, but she waited for Farah to check him into their group before hugging him.

From the tree they watched the firefighters work at the building. Later Solís told the adults that the crew had found the source of smoke in equipment inside the closed room; qualified staff would inspect everything before the center reopened.

Nobody went back for the models that night. Damián was sad about losing sight of his dragon, though he knew people came first.

Imani promised to make another if the paper one did not survive. This time the promise came easily.

## 10. After the sirens

The next day Farah called the families. Everyone had left safely, and the center remained closed for inspection.

With permission from those in charge, an adult later collected the undamaged models and found the dragon too, a little crumpled. Imani studied her cardboard building and saw a missing piece: she had drawn two doors, but no figures showed the adults bringing the groups together at the tree.

She added colored people markers and a place to keep track of arrivals. When Solís saw her working at the station, he said emergencies were not solved by one person racing ahead.

There were teams, decisions, communication, and reviews afterward. Imani told him how she had waited without knowing where Damián was.

The captain did not promise she would never feel afraid again. He said she had given precise information to people who could act.

That was part of the work too.

## 11. The fair with two exits

The school fair took place the following week in the gym as planned. Imani brought her cardboard community center.

Visitors placed markers in different rooms and tested what happened when a hallway was blocked. The game did not end when a marker left through a door: they had to gather and count the figures by the tree.

Camila, presenting her miniature veterinary clinic, moved one figure through the second exit and asked who would tell the first group. Abril noticed that a folding display could change the space even when the floor plan stayed the same.

Imani added both questions to the game. Damián placed his wrinkled dragon beside the tree; nobody needed to go back for it.

Elián and other children tried new routes. Imani did not tell the incident to look brave.

She wanted visitors to see that a plan helped when people practiced it, reviewed it, and knew when to ask for help.

## 12. The last marker

While clearing the table, Imani found a blue marker beneath the model. She set it with the others before packing away the game.

Damián asked whether she still wanted to be a firefighter now that she knew how difficult a real alarm could feel. “Yes,” she said.

She liked the engine and the helmets, but they were no longer the most interesting parts. She wanted to learn to work with others, study a place before she needed its plan, and listen when someone said a person was missing.

Solís stopped to say goodbye. Imani showed him the cardboard tree surrounded by markers and the exit nobody had counted before.

Damián lifted his wrinkled dragon; its wings were still open. Imani knew an emergency was not an adventure to seek out.

But if an alarm ever sounded again, she would remember that getting out, staying out, and sharing information could help everyone reach the same tree.
