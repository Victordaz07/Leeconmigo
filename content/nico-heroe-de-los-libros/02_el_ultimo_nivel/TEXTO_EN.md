# The Last Level

An adventure that needs three voices

## 1. An impossible explanation

Nico invited Alma and Teo to the library when their class project stalled. They couldn't even agree how big their model should be.

Nico brought out the blue book and told them about the lighthouse. Teo thought it was a joke until a drop fell from a dry page onto his drawing.

Alma asked them to open it with all three seated together. The city had changed.

Its canals remained, but some bridges ended in white space. Nico read the first sentence of the new story.

A paper door appeared before them. Teo stepped back; Alma wanted to go at once.

They agreed on two rules: no one would cross a doorway without telling the others, and anyone could ask to return.

## 2. The enemy's name

They found Sira in a strangely quiet neighborhood. People remembered how their stories began, but not how they ended.

At the square, a play stopped before its final act: every actor froze just as a choice had to be made. Sira showed them a page with a wide erased patch.

People said the Eraser lived in the library at the city's center and kept its endings. Nico wanted to charge straight there.

Teo noticed a bridge fading beneath their feet. Alma pointed out a back street the neighbors could still use.

They helped everyone across first. As the last child reached safety, the bridge vanished altogether.

## 3. Three paths

The central library had three entrances: an unfinished bridge, a hall of mirrors, and a garden of locked doors. Nico favored the bridge; he knew how to find routes.

Alma suspected the mirrors hid a clue. Teo didn't want to split up.

They argued until the book shuddered and one entrance blurred. Alma took a breath.

She suggested trying each route together and drawing what they learned. In the garden they found keys of every shape, but none fit a lock.

Teo noticed that a door shifted whenever someone said what they hoped to find beyond it. The first one opened when he voiced his own idea.

## 4. The map's mistake

Back in the real world, their model had a problem too. The street Teo had measured wouldn't fit on its cardboard base.

Nico suggested cutting it. Teo was upset: it was the part he had worked hardest on.

Alma asked them to set the pieces down without glue and look from above. The street wasn't the problem.

They had placed the bridge on the wrong side. Nico admitted he had marked its position without asking.

They started a fresh base. As he wrote about it in his notebook, Nico noticed a pattern: in the paper city they had also wasted time defending routes before looking at the map together.

## 5. The hall of mirrors

They returned to the book the next day. Mirrors showed possible endings.

In one, Nico found the pages alone. In another, Alma solved everything without speaking.

In a third, Teo never crossed the door because he was frightened. Each child saw the fear they carried.

Nico wanted to cover his mirror. Teo stopped him. 'A possible ending isn't the ending we have to choose.' Each frame had an empty space where a decision belonged.

The three talked through what they would do if something went wrong. As they made plans aloud, the mirrors stopped showing fixed endings and revealed paths that could still change.

## 6. The Eraser

The final path led to a giant desk. Behind it waited the Eraser, a figure made of rubber crumbs and unfinished pages, not a monster with fangs.

With every sweep it rubbed out a question before anyone could answer. 'Then nobody can get it wrong,' it said. Hundreds of last paragraphs sat trapped in jars on its desk.

Nico grabbed one, but the sentence fell apart in his hands. Alma understood: they couldn't paste these endings back into place.

Each character had to choose what happened next. The Eraser pointed to three exits and promised to let them go if they stopped asking others to decide.

That sounded easy. The city's people would remain trapped.

## 7. The project becomes a clue

At school they worked on the display. Teo had made cards with two routes, but classmates always chose the one that looked like the right answer.

Alma changed the question from 'Which path wins?' to 'Whom would you help first, and why?' The replies were different, and each started a conversation. Nico thought about the Eraser's jars.

They didn't need one perfect ending. They needed to give people room to choose.

On a sheet they drew stations for the paper city: the theater, bridge, and garden. Beside each one, they left a space where a character could explain a choice.

It would take time to try. All three agreed to go back together.

## 8. An ending no one wrote alone

At the theater, Teo asked the actors what they wanted to do after the pause. One chose to search for a missing character; another lit the lamps and asked the audience for help.

The play moved on. In the garden, a neighbor chose to share her seeds, and a new path appeared.

Nico returned to the bridge and asked the boat crews where they needed planks. Each decision restored a little ground.

The Eraser tried to rub the roads away but couldn't reach every voice at once. Sira arrived with the neighbors and their own plans, some very different from the children's.

The jars opened. The Eraser shrank until it could fit in Nico's palm.

## 9. An eraser with a purpose

Nico nearly tossed the Eraser into the canal. Teo pointed out that an eraser could help fix a map without destroying the whole thing.

They left it in the mapmakers' workshop, where no one would have an ending chosen for them and anyone could revise a line. Sira showed them the lit lighthouse and a bridge that hadn't been there before.

They returned to school before their display opened. A corner of the model was crooked; they straightened it together.

When a visitor chose a route none of them had expected, Nico didn't correct her. He asked why, then added a path.

A shared project could grow when someone else noticed something new.

## 10. A door for others

The exhibit ended, but several children asked for another adventure. Alma suggested a reading corner at the neighborhood library, with maps and cards inviting others to invent routes.

Teo offered to build a portable shelf. Nico wanted to bring the blue book, though he didn't know if anyone else could enter it.

Grandma Lidia reminded him that a magic door wasn't required for a person to feel inside a story. They could listen, draw, and ask questions.

That night a fresh blank page appeared. It didn't look damaged.

It was waiting for a story they hadn't lived yet. In his notebook Nico wrote three names instead of one.
